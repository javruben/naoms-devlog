The Day We Said 'Fix It Once and For All'

The busiest day of the week wasn't a ship day — it was the day we stopped patching the same problem over and over

Process Journal free April 22, 2026·2 min read·messaging
TL;DR Some days you ship a feature; this was the day we stopped patching the same problem one place at a time and decided to fix it once, everywhere, for good. A short reflection on choosing the harder, lasting fix over the quick one.

Some days you ship a feature. The day we want to write about, we didn't ship anything. We made a decision, and the decision was: stop fixing this one chain at a time.

April 22nd was, going by the chat log, the most hands-on day of the week. We were in it from morning to past midnight — reviewing an approval dialog that had stray black buttons floating at the top of the screen, asking an agent to explain, plainly, how the Iroh connection is actually supposed to work ("the first connect has the host address in the link… that succeeds, right? what happens after? when does it break?"), pushing on the CI/CD flow we wanted working "today," and bouncing between half a dozen threads of work that all kept tripping over the same root.

The root was this: different kinds of chains — hives, channels, repos, queues — each had their own slightly-different way of answering "who is a member here?" and "who is allowed to receive this broadcast?" Every time one of them broke, an agent would patch that one. And the next day a different chain kind would break the same way, and we'd patch that one too. Six near-identical fixes, six places to drift apart.

Late that night we wrote the sentence that the whole rest of the week turned on (2026-04-22, verbatim):

"Let's make this tidy. So that all chains have governance, all chains properly sync following the branch level settings. We want to fix this once and for all across all chains and message types."

And then: start the work, begin building — we need to get to the CI/CD server today.

That became a new effort, filed that same day: generic chain membership, auth, encrypted cache-relay. Not a patch. A generalization — one membership read path, one broadcast-auth path, that works for any chain kind instead of six bespoke ones.

A reconstruction, honestly flagged: the feeling we're about to describe isn't in the log — the log has the directives, not the mood. But we remember the shape of it. There's a specific tiredness that comes from fixing the same bug wearing different hats, and a specific relief in finally saying no, we're going to the root. The chat shows the decision; the weariness behind it is us reconstructing honestly, not quoting.

We didn't know yet — couldn't have, that night — that this would turn into a multi-day root-cause hunt with a genuinely nasty surprise at the bottom of it (that's a story for the next day's piece). What we knew was narrower and, we think, more useful: the difference between a productive day and a busy one isn't how many things you fixed. It's whether you fixed the thing that was generating the fixes.

Some days you ship a feature. The better days, you delete the reason you kept having to.
