One per surface, and the fourth is the one that carried the stretch even though it added nothing you can screenshot.

A hundred cracks sealed, and two that held up the floor

The feature: none. This is the unglamorous half of the stretch, and it is the half that decides whether any of the other three are worth trusting.

Before: more than a hundred distinct defects were live across the system, each of them a way for something to fail that had already failed once.

Now: each has been traced to its actual cause, fixed there, and sealed with a permanent test that fails if the same crack reopens.

The shape of that work is the same roughly a hundred times over: find the root, fix the root, then nail it shut. It is not adding capability. It is making the capability already there refuse to fail the same way twice.

A fix without a test has an expiry date. Nothing stops a later change from quietly undoing a repair nobody wrote down as a requirement. The test is what converts "we fixed this" into "this cannot come back," and it is the only part of the repair that outlives the person who made it.

Files that could be written to disk in the clear

The first of the two serious ones. Files shared through a conversation could be written to disk unencrypted, slipping past the encrypted store that is supposed to hold everything at rest.

They now stay encrypted at rest, and the old unsafe path fails the checks on sight rather than merely being avoided by the new code. That second half is what makes it a fix instead of a correction: the wrong path is now refused, not just unused.

A background helper that could overwrite your identity

The second. A background helper could overwrite the keys that prove you are you — the kind of mistake that quietly locks a person out of their own identity with no obvious way back in.

That is now structurally impossible: the write is refused rather than permitted and audited. Neither of these two was a headline, and both are the sort of thing a system has to get right before it deserves anyone's trust.

A phone that is a home rather than a window

The feature: using NAOMS on your phone as the primary way in, not as a reduced view of the desktop.

Before: the mobile app was a smaller version of something else — the same screens, shrunk, with the desktop's assumptions about where you are and what you just did.

Now: your apps stack as swipeable cards under a single bottom toolbar that stays put, and each app remembers where you left it when you come back to it.

Your files come across too. You can browse them and open them right on the phone, which is the half of file handling people actually do on a phone most days.

The direction that is missing is worth naming. Putting a file up from your phone is still being built. For now the phone is where you go through your files, not yet where you add new ones — and that is a real gap rather than a detail.

What the canvas can and cannot do for you

Alongside the phone, the canvas grew a real set of hands: triangles, hexagons and stars, smooth curves you can bend by their handles, a freehand pencil, and connectors you draw from one thing to another.

Drag a photo straight off your photo library and it lands on the surface as a card. The AI sits alongside you here — it can hand you cards to place with your own hands, and it does not rearrange the canvas by itself. That fuller, hands-on partnership is still being built.

What this means, in plain terms

Five lessons this stretch paid for. Each came out of something that had already gone wrong, and each survives outside this codebase.

Guard the wrong path, do not merely avoid it

Files shared through a conversation could be written to disk in the clear. The fix did two things: it stopped the new code taking that path, and it made the path itself fail the checks on sight. Only the second one survives the next person who does not know the history. A rule enforced by everyone remembering it is not enforced.

Make the unrecoverable case impossible, not unlikely

A background helper could overwrite the keys that prove your identity, which locks a person out of themselves with no way back. It is now refused structurally rather than guarded by care. Where a mistake has no remedy, the correct amount of reliance on good behaviour is none.

A fix without a test is a fix with an expiry date

More than a hundred bugs were fixed at the root this stretch and then sealed with a permanent test each. The test is the only part of the repair that a later change cannot quietly undo without being told. Write down what must stay true, or you have written down nothing.

State the direction of anything that looks two-way

Messages from your outside networks arrive here and cannot yet be sent from here. An inbox that shows both sides of a conversation implies you can reply, because every other inbox in the world can. When a feature resembles a familiar one, its differences must be stated, not left to be discovered.

Separate what a machine can do from what it is trusted to do

Devices now describe themselves twice over: what each has detected it is capable of, and what you have deliberately declared it is trusted with. Capability and permission answer different questions and get confused constantly. A machine being able to do something is not an argument that it should.

The bug figure is the one number this stretch genuinely supports, and it is stated as "more than a hundred" rather than as a precise total, because that is the resolution the underlying record actually has.

For the same reason the areas below carry no per-area numerals. A number there would make the section look more rigorous while making it less true.

In one line

the same system got more capable and more trustworthy on every surface at once, and the part that mattered most was a hundred repairs nobody can see.

Three honest notes

  1. You cannot upload a file from your phone. Browsing and opening work. The phone is where you go through your files, not yet where you add them, and that gap is being worked on rather than designed away.

  2. The inbox reads but does not send. Messages from your outside networks arrive here and are tagged with their origin. Replying from here is still being wired, so this is a reading surface today.

  3. The canvas AI does not arrange the canvas. It can hand you cards to place with your own hands. The version that rearranges your surface for you is still being built.

Four things this stretch shows only partly, and what would settle them

Each of these is real and narrower than it will sound repeated back. Each names the gap rather than the task.

  • A hundred repairs is a count, not a trend. Every one was sealed with a test. Nothing here says whether the rate of new defects is falling, which is the only version of this number that would mean the system is getting sturdier.
  • The two serious fixes were found, not caught. Files written in the clear and overwritable identity keys were both discovered by people looking. Neither was surfaced by a check that would have caught it on the day it landed.
  • An inbox that only receives is half a claim. Reading every network in one list is genuinely done. Until replies leave from here, "one honest inbox" is a statement about arrival and nothing else.
  • The phone is a reader, not yet a place you put things. Browsing and opening files on the phone works. Until a file can go up from it, the phone cannot be the only device someone carries.
