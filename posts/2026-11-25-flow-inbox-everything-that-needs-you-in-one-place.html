Flow Inbox: Everything That Needs You, in One Place

One inbox for what asks for your attention, sorted into places, worked through card by card, by key or by voice, until nothing is left

Product Architect free November 25, 2026·4 min read
TL;DR Flow Inbox gathers what asks for your attention into one place and lets you work through it the way that suits the moment: as cards, as an overview of your day, one card at a time, or by voice while you drive. Every way ends the same way: with a clear line saying you are done, and what, if anything, is left for your screen.

Most of us spread our attention across a dozen apps. Messages here, mail there, news somewhere else, and a nagging feeling that something important slipped through. Flow Inbox is NAOMS's answer: one place for what asks for your attention, sorted so you can see what matters, and built so you can reach the end of it.

How you open it

Flow Inbox opens as its own window. At the top right of the screen there is also a small "Flow Inbox" pill, next to the ones for your wallet and approvals. Tap it and a pop-down shows just the next thing that needs you, with "Open Flow Inbox" to see the rest. When nothing is waiting, it simply says "All clear".

Places: where things belong

On the left, Flow Inbox sorts everything into places: Today, People, News, Explore, Goals, Decisions, Work and Admin, with Projects joining once you have one. On a phone the same list sits in a drawer behind a "Menu" button.

Two places work a little differently from a classic inbox:

  • People is ordered by relevance, not by date. The people who matter for what you are doing now come first. The Family view ends with the line "These are the people you are often in touch with, most relevant first."
  • News shows stories, not loose links. Each story card has a headline, a few highlights and the outlets that covered it, with one action: "See all perspectives".

Four ways to give attention

The same items can be worked through in four ways. You pick one from the menu, under "Way to give attention".

  • Cards. The default: your items as cards.
  • Overview. Your day on one page. It greets you, gives the date, and counts what is left, what is urgent and what is getting closer. Below that, "Today" holds what needs you, "This week" holds what can wait for its date, and at the bottom sit what Flow Inbox could not confirm and things that are only there so you know. On a quiet day it says "Nothing needs you today."
  • Process. One card at a time. You see a card, act on it, and move on. "Done" clears it, "Later" brings it back tomorrow morning, "Next" passes it by, and "Open in app" takes you to where it came from. Each action also has a single key, so you can go through a whole inbox without touching the mouse.
  • Voice. For when your eyes are busy, such as on a drive. More on that below.

Reaching the end

Flow Inbox is built around finishing, and every way says clearly when you are there.

Overview ends with "That's everything that needs you, on this" device. Process ends with a summary that is careful with the truth. It says "All 12 done" only when you really did all twelve. Otherwise it says "You went through all 12", followed by a tally of what you did, put off and passed. It then lists what is "left for your screen", grouped by why. Some came from senders it cannot confirm, some only let you know, and some you passed with "Next" are still in your inbox.

Listening instead of looking

The Voice way opens as a drive: audio only, card by card. It starts with a short spoken opener, something like "Good morning. 105 things came in. 17 need you. About 9 minutes. Shall we?" You answer "yes" to start or "stop" to stop. The minutes are an estimate, and the screen says so.

For each card you can go to the next, hear more, be reminded later, or send it to your screen. At the end it tells you what is waiting: "That's everything. Nothing waits for your screen," or how many do, and which comes first.

Some things are never read aloud, however convenient it would be. These are codes, payments and forms, and anything from a sender Flow Inbox cannot confirm. Those go straight to "Left for your screen", with the reason, for example "Flow Box can't confirm the sender. Open it in Mail."

Faces you can trust

A card can show the sender's photo instead of a letter. Flow Inbox only does this for a sender it has verified, and it matches the photo by the sender's NAOMS identity, never by a name or an email address. Someone who fakes a "From" line does not get to borrow a friend's face. Anyone it cannot verify keeps the plain letter tile.

The same Flow Inbox ships in the Android and iOS apps.
