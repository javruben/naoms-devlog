Dispatch · Week 16: Your Whole World on One Canvas — and Surfaces That Won't Lie to You

The apps stopped being separate screens and became things you arrange on one infinite canvas — while every surface got stricter about telling you the truth: a chat that starts honestly empty, a photo search that stays silent rather than fake a match, files whose revoke button admits what it can't undo, and a conversation list that structurally can't lose a room.

Process Dispatch free July 15, 2026·3 min read·meta
TL;DR This stretch was about two things at once: giving you one spatial place to hold everything you own, and making each surface refuse to overstate what it can do. The browser app is now an infinite canvas you drag your photos, files, and people onto; chat gathers your people and hives into one calm list; photo search runs on your own machine and stays quiet on the lanes it can't yet answer; the Files app can cut off access and tell you honestly what that does and doesn't do.

Some weeks are about adding a feature. This one was about a posture. Two of them, really, running side by side: give you a single spatial place to hold everything you own, and make every surface honest about the exact edge of what it can do.

The chart above is the raw motion — a busy nine days of sustained output that crested in the middle of the week. But commit counts are the least interesting thing that happened. Here is what actually landed, and where you can read the real story of each.

One canvas instead of a dozen screens

The browser app no longer opens onto a screen. It opens onto an endless, zoomable canvas. Your apps open as windows on it; you can drag a photo, a file, a person, or a note straight out of its app and drop it on the surface as a card, draw and arrange freely around your things, and pull an AI chat window over to talk through whatever you're looking at. It's the shell you land on the moment you sign in — and we're careful to say the on-canvas AI is a partner you call over today, not one that rearranges your canvas by itself yet. Full story: Your Whole World on One Infinite Canvas.

Chat that starts honestly empty

The rebuilt chat surface gathers the people and hives you talk to into one calm list, each person folded into a single row no matter how many ways they reach you. Nothing is read by an AI unless you invite it, per channel. And it starts honestly empty: outside networks appear only once you truly connect one, and the surface says out loud when a network — Signal — can't be connected yet, rather than faking a crowd. Full story: One Calm Home for the Conversations You Keep Close.

A photo search that would rather show you nothing

Type a place or a word and your whole photo library narrows at once — river, map, and timeline together — worked out entirely on your own machine, with nothing uploaded. The honest part is the restraint: the searches that need a model to read your pixels stay silent until you install that model, instead of inventing a confident-looking wrong answer. Full story: Search Your Own Photos on Your Own Machine.

Files that admit what a revoke can't undo

Your files live encrypted on your own machines and stream in from every device you trust at once — no cloud ever holds them. When you cut off a person's access, the Files app refuses the usual comforting lie: it tells you plainly that their access drops at the next rotation, not the instant you click, and that bytes already delivered stay readable. The truth is printed on the button. Full story: Your Files Stay on Your Own Machines.

A conversation list that structurally can't lose a room

Under the chat surface, your conversation list isn't a stored list that can drift out of sync with reality — it's rebuilt from the signed record every time you open it, by asking one honest question: which conversations do you belong to? A kept list can silently forget a room you were added to; a list that's re-asked every time cannot. Full story: The Inbox That Can't Quietly Lose a Conversation.

The through-line

Put those together and the week has a shape. On one axis, everything you own is moving into a single spatial place you arrange with your own hands. On the other, each surface is getting stricter about the line between what it does and what it merely looks like it does — an empty room over a fake one, a silent lane over a guessed answer, a revoke that confesses its own limits. A more capable system and a more honest one, in the same nine days. That's the version worth shipping.
