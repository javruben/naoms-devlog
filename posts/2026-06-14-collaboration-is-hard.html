The Workshop That Decided Together and Didn't Fall Apart

A small scene from the world we're building toward — a group strong enough to act, free enough that no one is captured, walking the narrow road between the tyrant and the mess

Vision Philosopher free June 14, 2026·2 min read·vision
TL;DR A small ordinary evening from the world where a group of people genuinely decides and acts together — no boss on top forcing it, no endless stalemate either. The hardest thing humans try to do, shown working: a we that doesn't cost anyone the I.

It's a Wednesday evening a few years on. The shared workshop — fourteen makers who pool a laser cutter, a welder, and a corner of a warehouse — needs to spend the month's pooled funds, and there's a real disagreement about it.

Half want a second cutter. Half want to fix the roof that leaks over the wood bench. There's no owner to break the tie, and that used to be the moment groups like this died. One way, somebody appoints themselves the decider "just for efficiency," and a year later there's an admin who controls the funds, holds the keys, and can lock anyone out — the group that started as equals quietly grew a boss. The other way, nobody can decide anything without everyone agreeing forever, so nothing gets fixed and the leak just spreads.

Tonight it goes neither way. Mara puts up a proposal: roof first, cutter next month if the supplier credit holds. It's visible to all fourteen. People weigh in — two object, one proposes a split, the welder points out the roof will cost double if they wait. The proposal gets reshaped in the open, and when it passes, it passes by the rule they all agreed to long ago, not by anyone's force. The funds move. No one person ever held them. No one could have quietly drained them.

What makes it work is unglamorous and load-bearing: the workshop can remember, decide, and act as itself, with no one in the middle, and every member stayed free to object out loud and to walk away whole if they ever wanted. The record of who agreed to what only grows forward; nobody can rewrite tonight into a different story next week. There's no back door, because a back door is just the tyrant ditch with the lights off.

It isn't frictionless. The maker who wanted the cutter is a little sore. Exit isn't costless — leaving means leaving the welder behind. We don't pretend those away. But the roof gets fixed, by fourteen equals, and on Thursday the wood bench is dry.

Here's what the evening quietly reveals: the hardest thing people try — trusting each other enough to act together without surrendering to a boss or dissolving into a mess — isn't impossible. It's just narrow. Most groups swerve into a ditch in the first mile. This one stayed on the road.

An honest word: not all of this evening exists yet, and the road is genuinely hard — a bad-faith member can still gum up the works, real exit still isn't free. Some of this runs today; some is the direction we're walking, one verifiable step at a time, in the open, with the unsolved parts left visible. We'd rather show you the we that doesn't cost you the I than promise we've already perfected it.
