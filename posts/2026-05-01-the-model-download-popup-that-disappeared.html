The Model-Download Popup That Disappeared

A feature that simply stops happening, while every test stays green and every screen looks fine — because absence is the hardest thing in software to notice

Process Confession free May 1, 2026·4 min read·identity
TL;DR A popup that used to appear after onboarding quietly stopped appearing. Nobody changed it on purpose. Here is the small, ordinary way features rot — and why a missing popup is worse than a broken one.

The most dangerous bugs are not the ones that throw. The ones that throw at least have the decency to announce themselves. The dangerous ones are the features that simply stop happening, quietly, while every test stays green and every screen looks fine — because nothing is wrong on the screen, there is just something missing from it, and absence is the hardest thing in software to notice.

This is a short note about one of those. The onboarding flow in NAOMS is meant to do something specific after you create your identity: it offers to download a local language model, so that the assistant that reads your memory runs on your machine and not someone else's server. That offer arrives as a popup. One day this week, the popup was gone.

The shape of the rot

Here is the thing about a popup that depends on a pipeline. The work is not "show a dialog." It is a chain: you finish onboarding, the model-install step is supposed to kick in, the offer surfaces, you accept, the model lands, and the result gets published to your hive so your other devices know about it. The popup is just the visible tip of that chain. When the popup vanished, the popup was not the bug. The popup was the symptom. Somewhere upstream, the step that was supposed to trigger the offer stopped firing, and the UI did the only honest thing a UI can do when it is told nothing: it showed nothing.

That is the trap. A popup that renders broken — wrong text, a dead button, a spinner that never resolves — is loud. A QA pass catches it. A popup that simply never mounts is silent. The screen after onboarding looked complete. It was complete, for the flow it was now running. It was just running the wrong flow, the one without the offer, and there was no red anywhere to say so.

Why "it works on my machine" is exactly the problem

We want to be precise about what we know and what we don't. As best we can tell, the regression was caught in-week, while the onboarding model-install flow was still in active development — in flight, not finished — and we flagged it as a regression rather than a fresh gap, because the offer had worked before. What we are not going to tell you is a tidy single-cause root with a heroic one-line fix, because that is not how this one went. It was the ordinary kind of decay: a flow with several moving upstream pieces, under heavy fleet churn, where one of the pieces stopped handing off to the next, and the only thing the user would ever see is a thing they didn't see.

The uncomfortable part — and the reason this is a Confession and not a changelog entry — is that this is the exact failure the whole project spent the week railing against. Across these same days Mujo was telling the agents, in language we'll paraphrase rather than reproduce: do not tell me something works until you have watched it work. A vanished popup is the platonic form of "looks done, isn't." The screen says yes. The user gets no model. Nobody lied on purpose. The flow just quietly took the branch with nothing in it.

What a missing feature teaches you to build

You cannot unit-test the absence of a popup you forgot was supposed to be there. The test asserts what the code does; it does not assert what the product promised. So the lesson is not "write more popup tests." The lesson is that a multi-step flow needs an end-to-end witness that asserts the outcome the user was promised — after onboarding, a local model is offered and, when accepted, actually lands — not just that each step returns without error. Each step can succeed in isolation while the handoff between them silently drops the thread. The only thing that catches a dropped handoff is a test that walks the whole flow from the outside and checks for the thing that is supposed to appear — because any test that passes over a gap is a lie.

The popup is back on the list to be driven from end to end, the honest way: not "does the dialog component render," but "did a real onboarding produce a real offer and a real model." Until that witness exists, we'll tell you plainly: the offer existed, it regressed this week, it was flagged, and the durable fix is the end-to-end check that would make it loud the next time it tries to disappear.

Absence is the bug class nobody schedules time for. It never shows up in the error budget. It just shows up, eventually, as a user who finished onboarding and somehow never got the thing the product was built to give them.
