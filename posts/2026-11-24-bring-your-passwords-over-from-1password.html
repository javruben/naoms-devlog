Bring Your Passwords Over From 1Password

The NAOMS vault can now read a 1Password export and save every item, sealed, as its own kind of secret

Product Architect free November 24, 2026·4 min read
TL;DR If your passwords, cards and notes live in 1Password, you can now bring them into the NAOMS vault in one go. You export a .1pux file from 1Password, pick it in the vault, check what was found, and NAOMS saves each item sealed on your own device. This is how it works.

Moving to a new place for your passwords is only worth it if you can bring the old ones with you. Typing in hundreds of logins by hand is not a real option. So the NAOMS vault now has an importer for 1Password, one of the most widely used password managers. You give it the export file that 1Password writes, and it brings every item across.

Where to find it

Open the vault, go to Secrets, and next to "New" there is now an "Import" button: "Import from 1Password". The vault has to be unlocked first, because every item is saved sealed, and sealing needs your vault open.

In 1Password, you export your data as a .1pux file. That is the file you pick here.

What NAOMS shows you before anything is saved

NAOMS first reads the file on your device and tells you what it found, before it saves a single item. The confirm step says something like "Found 312 items in 1Password", names the 1Password vaults they came from, and breaks the count down by kind: so many logins, so many cards, so many notes. It then says what will happen: "They will be saved, sealed, into your NAOMS vault, one by one. You can stop at any time."

It also tells you what will not come across, and why:

  • Items you already brought in from an earlier import are skipped, so importing the same file twice does not create doubles.
  • An item that appears twice in the file is brought in once.
  • An item that cannot be read is named, and the rest of the file still comes across.
  • Items in 1Password's trash are counted and left out.
  • Attached files stay behind. The screen says how many, and the items they belong to still come across.

Every item keeps its kind

1Password knows many kinds of item, and so does the NAOMS vault. A login arrives as a login, a credit card as a card, a passport as a passport. The importer maps more than twenty kinds, among them bank accounts, driver's licences, Wi-Fi routers, servers, email accounts, API keys, SSH keys, medical records and crypto wallets. Each item's fields land in the matching places of that kind. A field the vault has no place for keeps its 1Password label and is stored as an extra, hidden value, so nothing is lost. A field that had no label in 1Password is named by what it holds, such as "Text" or "URL".

Saving, one by one

When you confirm, NAOMS saves the items one at a time, through the same sealed path as adding a secret by hand. The screen counts along: "Saving 41 of 312…". If you stop, the item being saved finishes and the rest wait. Only one import runs at a time.

At the end, the result says exactly what happened:

  • "Saved 310 of 312 items from 1Password into your NAOMS vault."
  • How many were skipped because they were already there.
  • Which ones could not be saved, by name, with the advice to import the same file again to retry them.
  • If you stopped early, how many were not saved, and that importing the same file again brings in the rest.

Your passwords never leave your device

The whole import happens on your own device. The file, what NAOMS read from it and the plan of what to save are kept only for as long as the import runs, then dropped. Nothing from the file is written to the browser's storage. No password ever appears on the page, in a message or in a log. Messages only carry counts and item names.

There is one thing NAOMS cannot do for you. The .1pux file itself is not encrypted, so anyone who opens it can read every password in it. After a successful import, the screen says so: "Delete it now, and empty the trash."

Sensible limits

The importer takes up to 20,000 items from one export, far more than most people keep. The importer also checks the file as it unpacks it, so a damaged or oversized file is refused with a plain reason instead of filling up your device.

The importer is the same on the desktop, Android and iOS versions of NAOMS.
