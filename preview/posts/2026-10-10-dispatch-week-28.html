Four articles, and what else landed

The package store, from four angles

Four articles published on 5 October cover the package store from the person's side:

  • A store section for the channels that offer packages
  • A store you can look at, with a bug button on every page
  • A zip file becomes a website you can open in NAOMS
  • The install card now says where a package came from

Read them for the product-level story; this dispatch only counts and places the engineering around them.

What changed in the landings

Site consent and reserved app names

A run of landings under item 1973 tightened how installed apps are treated: setting a site's consent needs an issued grant plus a trusted click in the shell, installing a package refuses names reserved for built-in apps, and boot-time loading refuses chain-sourced manifests under reserved names. The browser-side changes were mirrored to the iOS and Android bundles.

Policy gate hardening

Under item 1947, landings made the runtime refuse a pathway policy it cannot scope at publish or adopt time, and made policy results report honestly whether the gate was enforced. Item 1064 added a schema check that validates a request payload against the task's adopted schema before dispatch, refusing when it does not match.

Chat and AI-window fixes

Also under 1947: editing and retracting a chat message now send the right events and show a refusal if one comes back; a deferred outbound message is a refusal rather than a vague "queued"; a failed model-list refresh keeps the last good list; a wide docked AI window keeps its title bar and close button; and "Rescan Models" really rescans, with a visible failure if the scan fails.

Chat-connect: first work lands in the codebase

This is the first week work on connecting NAOMS chat to other messaging networks (WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram) appears. What landed is a new roadmap item (1975) with its backlog and design records: owner answers to the open design questions, and a draft of a "resident bridge" lane. It is opt-in and not in any build; this is planning and design work in the repository, not live messaging.

Calendar adapter

A calendar-adapter item (1216) had a check reclassification merged onto main. Work continued on the adapter; the landing subject says no more than that.

Where the week's landings clustered

Counted as landing-subject frequency (what the first-parent landing subjects talk about), not the per-commit file union earlier dispatches used: rust/spec, packages and install, import, hive channels, and browser, with chat-connect appearing for the first time. Rust and clean-room work is specification and build-in-progress, not shipped.

An honest note on this week's pace

Volume fell through the week, from 788 on 5 October to 225 on 10 October. That is a measurement of these seven days only. A large share of the landing subjects are merges of main into long-lived branches ahead of landing, which inflates merge counts without being separate pieces of work.

What would prove this week wrong

  • Landing subjects are a rough guide to theme. A subject naming an item does not say how large the change was; the clustering above could read differently by file or by line.
  • Chat-connect is not a product claim. If any of it is described as working messaging, that description is wrong: only design records and a draft lane landed.
  • The counts are over the post-rewrite history (the 1797 package-extraction rewrite rewrote git history), so they do not chain to the pre-September published dispatches; a different counting method would read differently.
