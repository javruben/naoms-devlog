Week 30

A heavy week of landings: a run of security-hardening and honesty fixes in the contacts, identity and import areas, onboarding fixes on the web and phone shells, and design-doc and spec work for the clean-room Rust conversion, which is specification and build in progress.

Three things changed this week

Refusals that name themselves

Many of the week's landings tighten what the system refuses and make the refusal say so. An invite link that would present the root identity instead of a relationship identity is refused by name before signing. A co-device or app registration is no longer auto-granted without the owner. A hive outbox row is flushed only to an entitled member; otherwise it is parked and named. Triples refused during an import are named and counted rather than silently dropped. These are small, specific changes, each described by its own landing; none of them is a claim that the surrounding feature is finished.

The contacts area and its people views

A long run of landings in the contacts area worked on the People view and its profile pane: ordering friends first, drawing a person by display name rather than an identity fragment, a profile Send that no longer depends on a form submit event, and a pile of test-harness fixes so those screens are exercised through the real phone path. Alongside it, landings made location-sharing and name-assignment behaviour more honest and owner-only where the subject says so.

Import, onboarding and the clean-room Rust work

Import landings this week touched the import hub, an erasure/tombstone path and the way refused data is reported. Web and phone onboarding saw fixes (keyboard and Back handling on the connect screen, a clean-up of an onboarding step). Separately, landings tagged with the clean-room spec lane (the 1986 conversion) arrived, along with some changes that reach into the Rust sources. That conversion is a specification and a build in progress; nothing here says a Rust daemon has shipped, paired with anything, or reached interop.

Where the week's landings clustered

Landing subjects this week most often name contacts, import, hive, devices, vault and store work, with the web tier and calendar behind them. Landings tagged for the clean-room Rust spec lane were frequent, and a docs/design-truth pass ran alongside. A caution on one theme: a keyword count for "rust" over these subjects is inflated, because many subjects are gate notes that say "0 rust" (meaning the change touched no Rust), so only a minority actually concern Rust sources. This is landing-subject frequency, read from the subjects of the merges that reached main this week. It is not the per-commit file union that earlier dispatches used, so it should not be compared with their area counts.

An honest note on this week's pace

This was a heavy week on the daily spread, peaking on 10-22 and nearly flat at the edges. Much of the volume is a land queue moving many already-built branches onto main, plus test repair, so the commit count measures how much reached the shared line, not how much was invented this week.

What would prove this week wrong

  • The "where the landings clustered" paragraph is a reading of landing subjects, not a measurement of effort. A subject can name a theme without the change being large, and a large change can carry a vague subject. Many landing subjects here are process notes (what was graded, what main was at) rather than descriptions of the change, so some themes are under-counted and some may be over-counted.
  • The what-changed sections describe what landing subjects say was merged. They do not claim any of it is complete end to end, turned on by default, or proven in use.
  • The counts are over the post-rewrite history (the 1797 package-extraction rewrite rewrote git history), so they do not chain to the pre-September published dispatches; a different counting method would read differently.
