What changed in the landings

There were no companion articles this week, so this dispatch is engineering-stats only, grounded in the landing subjects.

Linking a second device, and importing a chain

Item 1244 landings worked on the path where a new device joins an identity: the joining device's parked append now waits while the founder's chain import makes progress and ends only when it settles or stalls; a parked write waiting on a signer rolls its timeout from the last verified landing; and a test harness for device pairing gained a founder approval watcher. These are fixes and test harness work, not a claim that device pairing is finished.

Owner-local chains stay local

Item 866 landings made chains marked owner-local get served, pushed and announced only to the owner and the owner's own devices, with tests covering refusals by name. Related landings made chain erasure require approval and made erased revocations fail closed.

Hive channels and identity

Item 1975 landings fixed hive channels so an invitee works under their per-hive identity rather than their root one, made a joining member receive a membership credential for that identity, and fixed a chat feed that showed "0 channels" in every hive. New channels now share chat history with new members by default. Marketplace channel minting was tied to the hive steward's credential rather than plain membership.

Calendar and adapter test repair

A batch of calendar and adapter landings repaired tests and checks: awaited async enrichers, screenshot rules in end-to-end tests, and a new per-site sandboxed web-app design (item 1983) with install-time validation of a "formFactors" field.

Calls

A calling fix stopped the local sequencer from being treated as dropped out when a heartbeat stamp went stale during an approval wait.

Housekeeping

Design docs lost dead path references, environment-variable registrations were added for benchmark and default-on levers, daemon start and restart now forward CLI config flags, and several guards around desk and host tooling landed. Many landing subjects here are tooling and process, not product changes.

Where the week's landings clustered

Counted as landing-subject frequency, not the per-commit file union earlier dispatches used: import and adapters (the heaviest), devices, hive channels, rust/spec, install, calls, and calendar. Rust and clean-room work is specification and build-in-progress, not shipped.

An honest note on this week's pace

This week is higher in volume than week 28 (4,512 against 3,211 commits) with fewer merges (890 against 1,176), so a larger share is non-merge work. That is a measurement of these two windows; it says nothing about quality, and much of the volume is red/green test pairs and recuts of branches onto main.

What would prove this week wrong

  • Subjects name what a change is for, not how big it was. A different method, such as per-file or per-line weight, could rank the themes differently.
  • Hardening landings are not completion. Nothing above says device linking or any gated feature is done end to end.
  • The counts are over the post-rewrite history (the 1797 package-extraction rewrite rewrote git history), so they do not chain to the pre-September published dispatches; a different counting method would read differently.
