Your hive votes on your email check

Ask NAOMS to verify your email or phone and a panel of providers your hive trusts is drawn for you. One sends the code, the others audit, and every vote is signed on the hive's chain.

Product Architect free November 2, 2026·4 min read
TL;DR How GreenCheck verifies an email address or phone number with a community panel: a random draw from your hive's credentialed providers that excludes you, one provider sending the code, two auditors, and commit-then-reveal votes signed on the hive chain.

When you prove that an email address or phone number is yours, someone has to send you a code and someone has to confirm it came back right. Most services make that one company's job. GreenCheck gives it to your own hive: the providers your hive has credentialed do the work, and a panel of them decides.

An earlier post covered how the codes themselves are sent by email and text. This one is about who is chosen to send and judge them, and how that choice is made.

What you do

You open the verification for your email or phone, type the address, and press Send Code. The screen tells you what is about to happen: a community checker will email or text you a 6-digit code, that checker will see your address because it has to send the code to it, and two other checkers then check the first checker's record. A hive needs at least three credentialed checkers besides you for this to run.

Then you wait for the code and type it in. The rest happens between the providers.

Who gets picked

The pool is the providers your hive has credentialed, minus you. You can never be on the panel that checks you, and you never send or judge your own code.

The pick has to be fair, so no single provider is allowed to choose it. A few of the eligible providers each commit to a secret random number first, which means they publish a sealed fingerprint of it. Only after all of those commitments are visible do they reveal the numbers. The numbers are combined into one seed, and the seed decides the panel: one verifier, who sends the code, and two auditors. Because commit comes before reveal, nobody can adjust their number after seeing the others. Anyone in the hive can recompute the draw from the chain and see that the panel is exactly the one the seed produces.

Sending and checking the code

Your address is not posted in the open. Your device encrypts it so that only the chosen verifier can read it, and the chain carries only a fingerprint that lets everyone confirm later that the same address was used throughout.

The verifier opens the address, creates the code, and sends it by its own email or text channel. You type the code you received. Your device then records only a fingerprint of what you typed, not the code. After that, the verifier reveals the code it sent. The fingerprint can only match if you really held the code.

The vote

The verifier, and each of the two auditors, now judges the case from its own copy of the chain. Each one checks that the panel is the one it computed itself, that you are not on it, that the code sent was committed to before it was revealed, that your fingerprint matches the revealed code, and that the steps happened in the right order and within the time allowed.

Each panelist then votes in two moves, again commit then reveal. First it posts a sealed vote, so nobody can copy or react to another's. Once the sealed votes are in, each reveals its verdict. Two of the three decide the outcome.

Every one of these steps is an event signed by its author's own identity inside your hive and written to the hive's chain. That makes the whole check auditable after the fact: who was drawn, what each one committed, what each one revealed, and in what order.

What you are told along the way

The screen is careful not to claim more than has happened. After you press the button it says a checker has been asked to send your code. It says a checker sent it only once that checker has recorded the send itself. If a checker cannot send, perhaps because it has no way to deliver messages, the reason is named rather than left as an endless wait.

Two practical things are worth knowing. Each checker accepts up to three verification requests from one person in 24 hours. And this panel route is for email and phone; other kinds of proof are turned away by name and have their own routes.

What it guarantees

The person asking never sends or judges their own code. A provider your hive credentialed, who is not you, sends the code to the address you committed to. You answer with something only the holder of that code could produce. Three providers, drawn by a seed none of them controls alone, each vote with a signature that stays on the chain for anyone in the hive to inspect.
